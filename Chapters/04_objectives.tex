\chapter{Research Objectives}
\label{sec:research_objectives}

The primary aim of this PhD project is to improve informed decision-making in the diagnosis of ovarian cancer by updating and better understanding the \gls{ADNEX} model, ensuring that it can be used safely and effectively across diverse clinical settings. The project combines applied clinical research (Part~\ref{part:applied}) with methodological research (Part~\ref{part:methods}). Although the applied work centres on ovarian cancer diagnosis, the methodological insights are intended to be relevant more broadly to the development and evaluation of clinical prediction models.

The applied objectives are to update, and externally validate the \gls{ADNEX} model so that its performance becomes more robust and generalisable across settings. First, we systematically review and meta-analyse \gls{ADNEX} model performance across all available published evidence (Chapter~\ref{sec:chp_ADNEXSR}). Second, we compare head to head the \gls{ADNEX} model with the \gls{RMI} model to evaluate their relative performance (Chapter~\ref{sec:chp_ADNEXvsRMI}). Third, we update the \gls{ADNEX} model using insights gained during more than ten years of clinical use, together with newer data that include patients who were not operated (Chapter~\ref{sec:chp_ADNEX2}).

The methodological objectives are to improve the evaluation and development of clinical prediction models, with a particular focus on accurate predicted probabilities (i.e. calibration) and on the utility of the model when accounting for misclassification costs (i.e. net benefit). To achieve this, we investigate how different algorithms and hyperparameter settings affect predicted probabilities, including understanding overfitting and the generation of probabilities  in random forests (Chapter~\ref{sec:chp_RFOverfitting}) and exploring the uncertainty of individual risks (Chapter~\ref{sec:chp_FundamentalProblem}). We then develop calibration methods and calibration plots that incorporate clustering information, thereby enabling better evaluation of model performance in studies with clustered data (Chapter~\ref{sec:chp_ClusteredCalibration}). Finally, we explore how clinical utility can be incorporated into model development by designing a variable selection algorithm that prioritises clinical usefulness over purely statistical fit (Chapter~\ref{sec:chp_varselection}).

The expected outcome is a robust, well-calibrated, and clinically useful prediction model for ovarian cancer diagnosis with demonstrated utility, together with methodological advances that support informed decision-making across clinical prediction modelling more broadly.
