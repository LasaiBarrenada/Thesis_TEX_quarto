\section{Background}

Patient healthcare management involves a series of complex decisions that clinicians must make to provide optimal care. Determining which decision to take is often not straightforward, as it requires weighing the potential benefits and harms of different options in an environment with limited resources. Informed decision-making is crucial to ensure that patients receive the most appropriate care while minimizing unnecessary interventions and associated costs. In the \gls{AI} and big data era that we are currently living, the amount of available data is not processable by humans, and clinicians are increasingly relying on data-driven tools to support a well informed decision-making. 

To support clinicians in this process, various tools and frameworks have been developed, including clinical prediction models, which are designed to estimate the probability of specific outcomes based on patient characteristics and clinical findings. These models can assist clinicians in making evidence-based decisions by providing personalized risk assessments that guide diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment strategies.

The role of biostatisticians and methodologist in this new landscape of data-driven decision support tools is crucial. From study design to model implementation in clinical practice, all steps are important to ensure that the developed tools are accurate, reliable, clinically useful and fair.  
In this thesis, the focus is on the development and evaluation of clinical prediction models for diagnosing ovarian masses, a common clinical problem with significant implications for patient management.  


\section{Clinical prediction models}

Clinical prediction models are statistical tools that combine multiple predictors to estimate the probability of a specific outcome for an individual patient \cite{steyerbergClinicalPredictionModels2009}. These models are widely used in medicine to assist clinicians in making informed decisions about diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment. This thesis will focus on prediction models for diagnosis. By integrating various patient characteristics and clinical findings, prediction models can provide personalized risk assessments that guide clinical management. Succesful and widely used clinical prediction models include the Framingham Risk Score (cardiovascular disease) \cite{dagostinoGeneralCardiovascularRisk2008}, the APACHE II score (critical care) \cite{knausAPACHEIISeverity1985}, and the \gls{ADNEX} model (ovarian masses) \cite{vancalsterEvaluatingRiskOvarian2014}, which gives this thesis its title.
